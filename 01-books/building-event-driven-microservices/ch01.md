# Chapter 1. Why Event-Driven Microservices

## What are Event-Driven Microservices?

Microservices and micro-styled architectures have existed for many years, in many different forms, under different names. 

In modern event-driven microservices architecture, system issuing and consuming events.
These events are not destroyed upon consumption as in message-passing systems, but instead
remain available for other consumers to read as they require.

The services are small and purpose-build, created to help fulfill the necessary business goals
of the organization.

These services consume events from input streams, apply their specific business logic.
And may emit their own output events, provide data for request-response access, communicate with 
a third-party API, or perform other required actions.

## Introduction to Domain-Driven and Bounded Contexts

Domain: The problem space that a business provides solutions to.
This includes rules, processes, ideas anything related to its problem space.

Subdomain: A component of the main domain. Each subdomain focuses on a specific subset of
responsibilities.

Domain model: An abstraction of the actual domain useful for business purposes.

Bounded context: The logical boundaries, including the inputs, outputs, events, requirements,
processes and data models, relevant to the subdomain.

Bounded context should be highly cohesive. 
The internal operations of the context should be intensive and closely related, with the vast majority of
communication occurring internally rather cross-boundary.

Connections between bounded contexts should be loosely coupled, as changes made within one bounded context
should minimize or eliminate the impact on neighboring bounded contexts.
